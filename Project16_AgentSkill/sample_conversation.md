# Sample conversation: `/viva [photosynthesis notes]`

**Source excerpt provided by the user**

> **Photosynthesis (page 12):** In the light-dependent reactions, chlorophyll absorbs light energy in the thylakoid membranes. Water molecules are split, releasing oxygen, electrons, and hydrogen ions. The electron transport chain helps build a proton gradient, which powers ATP synthase to produce ATP. NADP+ is reduced to NADPH.
>
> **Calvin cycle (page 13):** In the stroma, carbon dioxide is fixed to RuBP by rubisco. The cycle uses ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent reactions to produce G3P. Some G3P can be used to form glucose; the remaining molecules regenerate RuBP.
>
> **Summary:** The light-dependent reactions convert light energy into chemical energy carriers. The Calvin cycle uses those carriers to fix carbon.

**Assistant:** Question 1/5 — In which cell structure do the light-dependent reactions take place?

**User:** The thylakoid membranes.

**Assistant:** Feedback: Correct. The light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes.  
Question 2/5 — What roles do ATP and NADPH play in the Calvin cycle?

**User:** They supply energy and reducing power to help convert fixed carbon into G3P.

**Assistant:** Feedback: Correct. ATP supplies energy, and NADPH supplies reducing power for the Calvin cycle.  
Question 3/5 — If the light-dependent reactions stop producing NADPH but the Calvin cycle still has ATP available, what would happen to G3P production, and why?

**User:** It would slow down because the cycle would lack NADPH, although ATP remains available.

**Assistant:** Feedback: Partly correct. G3P production would be limited because the cycle requires NADPH as well as ATP; it could not continue normally with ATP alone.  
Question 4/5 — A plant is given carbon dioxide, but its rubisco activity is blocked. Which Calvin-cycle step is disrupted, and what is the consequence for the cycle?

**User:** RuBP regeneration stops.

**Assistant:** Feedback: Not quite. The blocked step is carbon fixation: rubisco normally helps attach carbon dioxide to RuBP. This prevents the cycle from producing G3P normally.  
Question 5/5 — Explain how the light-dependent reactions and the Calvin cycle depend on one another, naming the exchanged molecules and the role of carbon dioxide.

**User:** The light reactions make ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle uses both and carbon dioxide to make G3P, and then returns ATP and NADPH to the light reactions.

**Assistant:** Feedback: The first part is correct: the Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide and produce G3P. The carriers are used in the cycle rather than returned unchanged to the light-dependent reactions.
